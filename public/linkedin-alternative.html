Why LinkedIn fails founders specifically

The problems are structural, not superficial — they cannot be fixed with better search habits or a Premium subscription.

LinkedIn is optimised for a fundamentally different use case

LinkedIn's business model depends on recruiter subscriptions. Talent Solutions — the product line that sells to corporate recruiters — generates the majority of LinkedIn's revenue. This is not a criticism; it is the architectural reality. Every product decision LinkedIn makes is, at some level, filtered through "does this serve the recruiter use case?" This means the platform is structurally optimised for job-seeking and corporate hiring — not for the co-founder search, investor discovery, or peer founder networking that early-stage founders in India actually need.

No co-founder intent filter. You cannot search LinkedIn for "technical founder actively looking for co-founder, Bengaluru, within 10 km." You can search by job title — "Software Engineer" — but a job title tells you nothing about whether someone is ready to leave a well-paying role to co-found a startup. The result is that co-founder outreach on LinkedIn is an exhausting guessing game. You are cold-messaging people who have given no signal of their openness. Response rates for this kind of outreach are consistently under 5%.

The algorithm actively hides non-connections. LinkedIn's discovery algorithm prioritises content from people already in your network and from accounts that pay for visibility. Someone who has recently started looking for a co-founder opportunity in your city but is not in your existing network is essentially invisible to you. You can only find them through paid search features — which still give you no co-founder intent signal. You know they exist in the city; you do not know if they are looking for what you need.

Recruiter noise degrades every channel. LinkedIn's inbox is flooded with recruiter InMails for most professionals with 3+ years of experience. This means your genuine co-founder outreach message competes with 20 job offers for attention in the same inbox. Even when founders do find potential co-founders on LinkedIn, they routinely report that their messages go unread — not because the person is not interested, but because the message looked like one of fifty recruiter messages that week.

The solution is not to use LinkedIn better — it is to use a platform that is built around the actual founder use case. Intent-based platforms require every user to declare what they are looking for before they become discoverable. When both sides have declared their intent, the first message has immediate context, and response rates reflect that difference.
